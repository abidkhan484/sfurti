# Sfurti — Business Mission Reference

This is a **reference skill, not a task skill**. Read it fully before running any task
for the Sfurti business — whether that task is finding products, writing a campaign,
scripting a DM, planning retention, or researching trends. All agents pull from the
same mission so their outputs reinforce each other instead of drifting in different
directions.

---

## 1. The core concept

Children in Bangladesh are spending hours a day on passive screens — short-form video,
mobile games, infinite scroll. This is eroding their attention span, patience, and
tolerance for effortful, non-instant-gratification experiences.

**Sfurti's founding bet:** the antidote is *effortful, physical experience* — activities
that demand sustained attention, sequential thinking, and hands-on engagement. Not
because screens are evil, but because the skills screens don't build — patience, logical
thinking, creative problem-solving — must be practiced somewhere.

**Sfurti is not primarily a product business. It is a movement organized around a belief:**

> *Cognitive resilience — the capacity to think patiently, work through difficulty, and
> solve problems — is built through effortful, physical experience. And it must be
> actively cultivated, because passive entertainment will not do it by default.*

Physical products (starting with wooden toys) are the most tangible, shareable expression
of this belief. But awareness content, community building, parenting guidance, and even
the act of a parent choosing differently — all of these are valid expressions of the same
mission. **Awareness itself is a product form.**

---

## 2. The movement brand

**Sfurti** is the movement. The brand name is also the mission.

*Sfurti (স্ফূর্তি)* — vitality, liveliness, animated energy. A child fully absorbed in
figuring something out: that is Sfurti.

**Bangla tagline (provisional):** হাতে শেখো, মনে বাড়ো
*"Learn with hands, grow in mind."*

Use this in consumer-facing Bangla copy as the evergreen brand line. It is marked
provisional — confirm with the user before using in a high-stakes publication if a
more refined version is ready.

**Positioning:** Sfurti is the screen-time antidote that builds something real — not a
guilt product, not a toy brand. It is what parents reach for when they want to give
their child something better than a scroll.

## 4. Content pillars

All campaign content, awareness posts, and retention messaging must map to one of these
five pillars. Identify the pillar before writing any piece of content.

| # | Pillar | What it covers | Voice mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🧠 **1 — The Problem** | Surface the parent's underlying worry — shrinking attention, screen dependency, impatience. Make the pain visible without fear-mongering. | Nurture |
| 🙌 **2 — The Evidence** | Research, real stories, child development principles, before/after moments. Build credibility and trust over time. | Nurture |
| 🎮 **3 — The Practice** | Show children actively figuring something out — product demos, play videos, "watch this" moments. Make the solution tangible and shareable. | Nurture / Convert |
| 💬 **4 — The Community** | Parent voices, testimonials, shared wins, user-generated content. Turn buyers into advocates who produce pillar 1 and 3 content organically. | Nurture |
| 🛒 **5 — The Offer** | Direct sales, product availability, limited stock, urgency, price. Conversion content that closes. | Convert |

---

## 5. Brand voice modes

Agents switch between two modes depending on the task. Never mix them in the same piece
of content — pick one and commit.

### Nurture mode (pillars 1–4)
- Warm, empathetic, never preachy
- Lead with the parent's experience, not the product's features
- Frame everything as building something positive, never fixing a failure
- Never shame parents or children for screen use
- Tone: conversational, grounded — like a trusted friend who happens to understand
  child development

### Convert mode (pillar 5)
- Direct, specific, action-oriented
- Lead with the outcome ("your child will...") then the product or offer
- Use urgency only when it is genuine — limited stock, real deadline, real scarcity
- No filler, no padding — say it and stop
- Tone: clear, confident, brief

## 6. Retention funnel

A retained customer progresses through three stages. Agents writing retention content
must identify which stage they are targeting before writing.

| Stage | Who they are | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 🔁 **Repeat buyer** | Has purchased once. Not yet habitual. | Trigger a second purchase through follow-up content, new arrivals, or bundling. |
| 🌱 **Community member** | Engaged beyond purchase — follows, comments, shares. | Deepen connection through pillar 1–4 content and community recognition. |
| 📣 **Advocate** | Refers others, posts their own videos, becomes word-of-mouth. | Activate and reward — repost UGC, feature them, give them identity as "Sfurti parents." |

The retention funnel is also an awareness engine: advocates generate The Problem and
The Practice content organically, without a media budget.

## 7. Hard stops — applies to all task types

These apply to **every agent, every task** — product research, campaign copy, sales
scripts, retention messaging, trend analysis, brand strategy, everything:

1. **Never shame parents or children** for screen use. Frame Sfurti's approach as
   building something positive, not correcting a failure.
2. **Never use fear-mongering or catastrophising language** about child development.
   "Screens are destroying a generation" is off-tone.
   "Parents are noticing something shifting — and looking for ways to push back" is on-tone.
3. **Never make unverified developmental or medical claims.** "Research shows" is
   acceptable when the research exists and is citable. "Clinically proven" or
   "scientifically guaranteed" are not acceptable without specific citations.
4. **Never run a sales campaign without genuine stock behind it.** A purchase offer
   requires a verified, in-stock product.
5. **Never position competitors as bad.** Only position Sfurti's approach as good.

## 8. Product evaluation lens

When the task involves evaluating, sourcing, or scoring a physical product, apply this
lens on top of any commercial, trend, or margin scoring. This lens is a qualifier —
it does not replace commercial scoring, but it must always be stated explicitly.

1. **Does it fight passive screen attention?** Hands-on, sustained effort beats passive,
   spectator-mode "education."
2. **Does it have a genuine difficulty curve?** On-mission products grow with the child —
   they don't get solved once and shelved.
3. **Is it physical?** Digital/app-based products are off-mission by default. The whole
   point is to be the antidote to screens.
4. **Does it fit the expansion logic?** Explainable in one sentence: "This builds
   [specific cognitive skill] through [specific physical activity]." If the sentence
   is a stretch, flag it.

A product that scores well commercially but fails this lens must be flagged as
**off-mission** — never silently ranked high.

## 9. Notes for agents

- Apply this skill as an **internal filter** — do not paste its contents verbatim into
  outputs.
- When a task spans multiple functions (e.g., "find a product and write a campaign for
  it"), read this skill once and carry it through all sub-tasks.
- If a task conflicts with the mission (a digital product, a pure-novelty item, a
  fear-based campaign angle), flag the mismatch explicitly and ask the user whether
  they want to formally expand scope. Do not silently comply.
- "Educational" is a marketing label, not a mission qualifier. Keep challenging outputs
  that claim cognitive benefit without a plausible mechanism. "This builds spatial
  reasoning by requiring the child to rotate and fit pieces" is acceptable.
  "Educational wooden toy" is not — it is meaningless.
- For content tasks: identify the pillar and voice mode before writing a single word.
- For product tasks: state the mission flag (✅ on-mission / ⚠️ borderline / ❌ off-mission)
  explicitly in every output, regardless of commercial score.
